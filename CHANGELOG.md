# Changelog

All notable changes to ABC Desk Web are documented in this file.

This project follows a lightweight [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) style.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- About has her on it. The character from the author's blog looks over the edge of the sheet: half a head of the blog's own drawing — the `Head` and `Hair_Accessory` groups of `Character_Standing.svg`, copied path for path, hair ornament and fringe intact — with the masthead's note worn on top of it, in the masthead's ink and built from the same three polygons (`brand-mark.test.ts` now compares four copies of the mark rather than three). A window does the cropping — a box that stops just under the nose, so a still face has no mouth to show, and that ends on the line the header already draws as its bottom edge, so the cut reads as the paper's edge rather than as a picture trimmed to fit; her viewBox is left long enough to hold the chin — and the mouth, drawn where the blog draws it, 11 units under the line — that her hover rise has to find. She blinks the blog's blink — both eyes squashed towards their lids and open again in 170ms, at the blog's own irregular 2.2–6.4s, with the odd double blink 280ms behind — one deliberate difference from the blog: its eye groups also hold the brows, so a blink drags them 26 units down onto the lids, which on a 64px face reads as the eyes falling rather than closing; here the four brows sit in a group of their own and stay where they were drawn while the lids squash about 154, the middle of the pupil and the lash — and the timers only run while the sheet is open, since a face blinking behind a closed dialog is work nobody asked for. A reader who asked for reduced motion gets a still face. She is also the way back to where she comes from: the head is an anchor to the author's homepage (`https://maribbit.com/`), opened in a new tab so the draft behind the sheet survives the trip, and since the drawing says nothing the anchor carries the name — the SVG stays `aria-hidden`. She comes up over the edge on hover — not scaled but raised 4px behind that window, with a strip of drawing kept below the crop, so the next of her chin meets the line, the mouth comes up over it and no paper shows through (a plain lift, with nothing kept below the crop, would carry her flat chin-edge up and leave a gap) — and takes the same focus ring as the buttons, drawn as an arch over her head; the sheet still opens with the close button focused, because a link that leaves the page is not where a reader should be dropped. Her neck and the shadow under her chin were left with the blog, because the crop hides them for good; her mouth was kept — drawn but under the line — since it is what the rise is for, and a test holds the crop between the nose and it and the rise long enough to clear it.
- The space bar plays and pauses. It is the transport's own button pressed for the reader — `toggleTransportPlayback` clicks `.abcjs-midi-start` instead of keeping a second copy of the state machine, so the spinner while the soundfont loads, the restart at the end of the tune and the pause that takes the highlight down all stay abcjs's. It answers wherever nothing focused wants the key first: the source keeps its spaces (a space there is a note), and so do buttons, links, `summary` and the text fields — which means the reader can be looking at the sheet, the transport or the keyboard and still press space, while a modified space stays with the browser and the system. A tune stopped mid-phrase carries on where it stopped rather than starting again from the note last touched, and the play button's title now says so: `Play or pause (Space)`, with the plain action as its accessible name. About gives it its own scope, `Outside the source`.
- About now says how to print. The combination is the browser's own (`Mod-P`); what the desk contributes is the paper — the print stylesheet takes the panes, the header and the source away, so the printer gets the sheet and nothing else — so the row sits under `In the browser` rather than pretending to be a page-level binding.

### Changed

- The line-wrap switch moved out of the source header and into the source's own footer, in the right corner beside the diagnostics switch, and it now starts on. Wrapping is a way of reading the text rather than an action on the file, so it keeps company with the other view switch while the header keeps the file's own verbs; and a wrapped ABC line is one the reader can see whole, so the default is the reading, not the scrolling. The markup carries `checked` and the editor starts from `DEFAULT_EDITOR_WRAP`, so the first paint is already wrapped instead of snapping into place once the stored settings arrive.
- The masthead reads `ABCDESK`, one word and no space: a wordmark for the note beside it, not the app's name. The name itself is unchanged — `ABC Desk Web` in the tab, in About, in the link card and in the store listing, which is what readers search for and bookmark.
- The mark is a faceted note now, not a badge. The old one was a rounded tile with a five-stop gradient, a rim light, a drop shadow and an embossed glyph — on a bar whose every other shape is flat, and beside a blog drawn entirely in flat polygons and hard corners. The note is drawn the same way the blog draws its furniture: a diamond head laid on the staff, a bar for the stem that runs into its right tip, a parallelogram with a notch for the flag — three polygons, one colour, no plate behind them and no corner to round. The masthead writes it inline so it can take `--workspace-accent` through `currentColor` (an `<img>` cannot), the tab's `icon.svg` is the same three polygons and flips its ink with the system theme (`#4f6779` on paper, `#9ab0c2` in the dark, because a single mid-grey exists in neither), and `brand-mark.test.ts` compares the points across the masthead, the tab and the install icon so the three copies cannot drift. The install icon is the one place the mark keeps a ground: a home screen has no desk to read a transparent glyph against, so 192 and 512 are the same note on a flat sheet of `#eef2f3`, generated from `scripts/icon-app.svg` while 16, 48 and 128 stay bare.
- The two play buttons stopped painting their press. The plate under the pointer was a rounded rectangle the size of the 28×34 box — and, on a phone, a 44px slab that a tap left lit — so the largest shape in the row was the one around a 15px glyph. Each button now fills a circle the size of the glyph: 28px on the desk, 32px inside the phone's 44px press. The keyboard's focus ring moved onto that circle too, because the browser's own outline was drawing the same rounded rectangle the eye had just been relieved of. Loading stopped turning the button as well: abcjs spins the element itself, so the press box and the lit plate went round with the ring — and that ring was filled solid, which made the one icon that is meant to turn look the same at every angle. Only the hollow ring turns now, and the button stays where the finger left it. Both buttons that answer a key now say which: `Play from cursor (⌘↵)` on Apple and `Play from cursor (Ctrl+Enter)` elsewhere, and the browser-fullscreen button adds `(Esc)` while it is the way out. The accessible names stay the actions' own — no reader should be asked to pronounce `⌘`.
- `Mod-Enter` now starts the tune from the caret wherever the reader's hands are, instead of only from the source. The caret is the workspace's — the sheet, the transport and the keys all read it — so the key was answering about focus rather than about the verb. It listens on the document now and leaves four cases alone: a sheet still being read (nothing plays behind About), an input method mid-composition, a field that owns the key (including the ones inside the player's shadow root, which retarget `event.target` to their host, so the check reads `composedPath()`), and the Shift and Alt variants. The editor keeps its own binding so a held modifier never inserts a line, and keys it has already claimed are recognised by `defaultPrevented` — without that, one press in the editor would play twice. About's table says which combination crosses the page: `Play from the caret` sits under `Anywhere`, and the three that act on the text sit under `In the source`.
- The About sheet became a short page that stays short. What the desk is takes one line, the four combinations the editor listens to sit right under it, and the two long answers — `One tune at a time` and `Converting MusicXML to ABC` — wait behind their own titles. Both are native `<details>` rows: the title line is the switch, the hint beside it already answers while the row is closed and steps aside when it opens, and Tab, Enter and Escape work the way the browser has always made them work. The key table ships the word `Ctrl` in the markup, so a page without script and a machine without Apple's keyboard both read something true, and the chips are rewritten to `⌘` at boot by asking `userAgentData.platform`, then `platform`, then the agent string. The drag-in, share-link and install-offline facts were pressed into a two-sentence footnote, the author and the GitHub link moved into a footer that now points at this repository instead of the extension's old one, and on a phone the two converter links and that footer link take a finger's height without moving the words they sit in.
- The desk opens as a reader's page on a phone rather than a shrunken desktop. Below 600px both bars hold one row at 360px: the header trades its wordmark for the controls, every icon and chip takes a 44px square, and the chosen chip is still drawn at its desk size inside that square — a 44px slab across a 56px bar read as a second toolbar. The bar also stopped wrapping when a state chip arrives: `Offline` and the update prompt take turns, and the prompt keeps only its button while the sentence stays in the live region for anyone listening. In the panes the tools a phone reaches another way step aside — copy and clear in the source header, browser fullscreen in the preview — and the transport's 10px rail still looks like a rail while a pseudo-element gives it the bar's height to be grabbed by. The keyboard's sideways drag ends with the keys instead of pulling the page after them.
- A page with no network says so: a quiet `Offline` label sits in the header next to where a new build would announce itself, and its tooltip only promises the saved copy when a worker is actually serving one. The samples are not the label's worry — playing offline after the install is unaffected.
- When the soundfont does not arrive, the transport says why instead of playing around the gap. A failure under the play button used to be invisible: abcjs drops the promise and keeps the button spinning, and a missing key on the piano only left a console line. Both now end as one muted sentence under the transport — offline it asks for a reconnect and a reload, online it asks for a reload — and the play button comes back instead of waiting on a load that never finishes.
- A new build is offered rather than imposed. The service worker installs and then waits instead of taking the caches over under an open page, and a small `New version ready` chip in the header reloads on the reader's click; the old shell and the samples it already has stay in place until then. The first install is not an update — there is nothing to replace — so it takes the page without asking.
- The URL hash now carries the tune deflated and base64url-encoded (`abc=z1.`), roughly a third of the percent-encoded text it used to hold, so a long piece still produces a link that fits in a message. Links written before this still open: the marker is what tells a deflated payload from plain text, and a browser without `CompressionStream` falls back to the old encoding rather than failing.
- A diagnostic no longer vanishes the moment you type. Its underline and margin dot ride the text until the next check replaces them, and the report panel stays open instead of collapsing and reopening under the caret on every keystroke; the status line says `Editing...` for that gap, because the report is about the text as it was. An empty sheet takes the last report down with it — no check follows an empty page, so nothing else would.
- The example tune now shows what a comment looks like in both places it can stand — on a line of its own and after the music — and names what a note's length number does.
- The source editor no longer reads and rescans the whole tune on every keystroke. The syntax greys follow the edit — a mark on untouched text stays on it — and only the lines the change reached are scanned again, so typing in a thousand-line pieces costs the same as typing in a short one; a fixed-seed test checks the patched layer against a full scan after every kind of edit. The change handler also reads the document once instead of three times, and the line count no longer builds a list of every line to count them.
- Play-from-cursor moved out of the source header and into the transport, left of the play button, with a new glyph — a triangle with a text cursor at its right, drawn in the target's blue — and it is offered only while a note is selected, because the shared target is what it starts from. The `Mod-Enter` shortcut still works from the editor. Its triangle is drawn at the same height as the play button's and both glyphs centre the same way, so neither button outweighs or sits above the other; on the light theme's bar the blue is deepened to `#0a7fd4`, where the sheet's `#1c9bff` would only reach 2.7:1 against the grey.
- The transport now sits above the sheet instead of below the keyboard, so the playhead stays where the reader is looking. Its restart button is gone — the progress bar's left edge is the one way back to the beginning — and its play/pause glyphs are drawn in the pane's own icon language (15px, 2px round stroke) instead of abcjs's filled shapes.
- The shared target is now drawn in a bright blue instead of the muted grey-blue, which was too close to the greys around it to read as a selection: the sheet fills the notehead and the dashed box with it, the source tints the characters' background and outlines them with it, and the keys of the target are filled with it. It stays one step lighter and colder than the playback blue, and only the target carries a box, so a note being read never looks like a note being heard.
- The caret and the score now share one selection. The note under the caret and the note you click are the same state, drawn the same way on both sides: a tinted background inside a dashed outline in the source, a filled note inside a dashed box on the sheet. abcjs's own click colour is neutralised at render time, so there is one selection language instead of three, and the source caret is collapsed rather than turned into a selection.
- The project is renamed to **ABC Desk Web** (`abcdesk-web`), the web counterpart of the ABC Desk VS Code workbench. Branding, package name, storage keys, custom element tags and CSS custom properties move from `chatmusic` to `abcdesk`.
- The "Studio" concept is renamed to "workspace" so the app name, the code and the docs share one word: `src/studio/` becomes `src/workspace/`, `docs/specs/studio.md` becomes `workspace.md`, and the related settings helpers, storage keys, CSS classes and custom properties follow.
- The Chrome extension target has been removed. ABC Desk Web is now a web-only ABC editor: the content script, ABC detectors, popup, background worker and MV3 manifest are gone, and the last extension state is preserved at tag `extension-v0.4.0`.
- The build now produces a single static site in `dist/`. `npm run dev`, `npm run build` and `npm run check` all target the Studio app, and `SITE_BASE` replaces `CHATMUSIC_WEB_BASE` for subpath deployments.
- Refined Studio responsive layout: compact header breakpoint at 1000px (tighter gap/padding) and the Layout Mode control hides at 860px, where auto mode forces the stacked layout.
- Unified icon sizes across the Studio About dialog (18×18 → 15×15) and gave the tempo icon an unfilled circle stroke, matching the project-wide Lucide/Feather icon language.
- The source editor is now a CodeMirror 6 view instead of a textarea with a highlight mirror underneath. The mirror — a second copy of the text that had to be kept perfectly aligned with the first — is gone, and the workspace keeps talking to the editor through the `AbcEditor` facade.

### Added

- `Cmd-P` prints the score, not the app. The bar, the source, the resizer and the transport stay on screen; the paper keeps the tune, its title and the directions above it, on white whatever theme the desk is wearing, and the target and playhead hand their ink back so a hollow notehead prints hollow. A tune longer than a page used to be cut straight through a staff — a page has no break opportunity inside one SVG — so before the sheet is laid out each staff system is copied into its own cropped SVG that keeps out of page breaks, and the browser breaks between systems instead. The pages exist only while the job is being laid out: `afterprint` puts back the single sheet the screen scrolls, and a re-render waits its turn.
- A link now has a face before it is opened. The tab names the tune — `T:` first, the app second — and falls back to `ABC Desk Web` when the sheet is empty or does not parse, reading the tune that reached the paper rather than the text still being debounced. The head carries a description, Open Graph and Twitter tags, and a 1200×630 card of the real workspace (`social-card.png`), so a pasted link arrives with something to look at rather than a bare address. The tune itself stays in the hash, where crawlers cannot read it, so the card promises the workbench and leaves the score to the reader. Previews that demand an absolute address get one from `SITE_URL` at build time, which also fills `canonical` and `og:url`. The address bar follows the resolved theme by reading `--workspace-bg`, so the chrome above the wall is the wall, not a second opinion about it, and with scripts off the page hides the interface it cannot run and says why.
- The workspace can be installed and opens without a network. A web app manifest, a 192 and a 512 icon, and a service worker that keeps the app shell and all 88 piano samples: the shell is cached all-or-nothing, so an offline open never lands on a page whose script is missing, while the samples are best-effort and live in a cache that survives deploys because they never change. Page loads stay network-first, so a deploy is seen on the next reload; everything else is content-addressed and answered from the cache. The worker is versioned by the list of files it holds and scoped to the deployment's own path, and it is registered only by a production build.
- `Copy Share Link` in the source header copies the tune itself as a link — no server, no account, no upload. Origin and path come from the page, so a subpath deployment shares its own address; opening the link lands in the workspace with the same music and then clears the hash, so a later refresh cannot overwrite what the reader has since written. The button is disabled on an empty sheet, where there is nothing to share.
- `Mod-/` toggles `%` comments over every line the selection touches, and takes them back when the block is already all comment. The blank lines inside a block are left alone — they would gain a run of bare `%` that has to be deleted by hand when the block reopens.
- A file dropped on the source pane opens the way the Open button opens it, so bringing a tune in is one gesture instead of three; the pane answers the hover with a dashed edge rather than moving the text, and a file dropped anywhere else is refused instead of navigating away from the workspace. An empty sheet now says `Drop an .abc file here, or start writing`.
- The source editor has a margin: line numbers that stay a reference rather than content (lighter than any grey the sheet paints the music in, with the caret's own number stepping up to the ink), a neutral tint across the caret line — never blue, since the target and the live note already own blue — and a dot beside the number of every line a diagnostic names, drawn in the diagnostics' grey-blue instead of a red light. The panel's "Line 7" now has something on the page to point at.
- Added a shared target for the two panes: the note under the caret and the note you click are one state. The sheet draws it as a filled note inside a dashed box, the source as a dashed outline around the characters, and the box lives inside abcjs's own SVG in user-space coordinates, so it stays on its note when the pane is resized (a chord is one box; each note of an expanded tie gets its own).
- Added browser fullscreen toggle as a complement to system fullscreen, expanding the player to fill the browser viewport without hiding browser chrome.
- Added Escape key support to exit browser fullscreen mode.
- Added syntax greys to the source editor: fields, comments and bar lines are dimmed so the notes stay the darkest thing on the page, and the sounding note keeps the only saturated colour.
- Added diagnostics to the source text: the ranges the quality panel reports are underlined with a thin grey-blue wave, and clicking a panel item (or pressing Enter/Space on it) selects that range and scrolls it into view.
- Added score-to-source pointing: clicking a note in the score makes it the target, moves the source caret onto the characters that note was written with (collapsed, not a selection) and scrolls them into view, without taking focus away from the score. The score's own reach decides what counts as a click on a note, so the mark follows the rectangle around a notehead, not just the glyph; clicking the paper around the staff lets go of the target in both panes.
- Added play-from-cursor: `Mod-Enter` in the editor starts playback at the note under the caret, or at the next note when the caret sits between notes.
- Added a play-from-cursor button to the Source header, drawn as a play triangle with a text-cursor badge; its tooltip names the shortcut for the current platform.
- Added cursor-to-score following: moving the caret marks the same note as the target and scrolls the staff line it belongs to into view, and a caret in the ABC header lines clears the target and returns to the top of the sheet. Nothing is played, highlighted or focused.

### Removed

- Removed Chrome Web Store release tooling: extension packaging script, version sync check, store listing, release checklist and manual QA checklist.
- Removed the remaining extension-era docs (dual build, AI assistance guide, conversion notes, quality tooling plan) in favour of a four-domain spec set.

### Fixed

- Fixed the app manifest arriving as a blob instead of a description of the app. EdgeOne maps MIME type by file extension and does not know `.webmanifest`, so the manifest was served as `application/octet-stream`; it is now `manifest.json`, which every static host reads as JSON. Chrome parsed either, but the file should not depend on the host's table.
- Fixed the line numbers sitting a couple of pixels above the lines they number. A 12px number in a 13.5px row stands on its own shorter line box, parked at the top of the row, so every digit floated; the margin is now a baseline layout aligned against a zero-width strut wearing the code's font, which keeps the two on one baseline whatever the font's metrics are. The same pair of metrics is declared once for the code and the strut, so they cannot drift apart.
- Fixed the caret just after a bar line pointing at the last note of the bar that had just ended. A bar line closes a bar, so the caret standing against one now looks at the next bar's first note; standing before the line still belongs to the bar that is ending. `|]`, `||`, `:|`, `::`, `[1` and the other spellings count as bar lines too.
- Fixed the shared target vanishing after the preview was resized. Every redraw hands abcjs a fresh click listener, and the claim that tells a note apart from the paper it sits on lived in the first render's closure: after a resize (or an edit) the container took a note click for a click on empty paper and cleared the target the same click had just set, so only the playhead moved. The claim now belongs to the instance, so every listener speaks for the same click.
- Fixed the keyboard losing the shared target. The keys of the note the panes point at are now filled in the target's colour, separately from the playback colour, so pointing at a note shows up on all three surfaces; a key that is both pointed at and sounding keeps the playback blue.
- Fixed the keyboard knowing nothing about the tune until the first sound. abcjs names the pitches of a timing event only after it flattens the tune, which it does when the soundfont arrives, so the keyboard had no notes to show before the reader pressed play. Flattening needs no audio and now runs before the first timing is read, so the tune's keys are marked from the first paint.
- Fixed a stopped score lighting up the note you clicked. Seeking fires abcjs's note callback even with the tune stopped, so the clicked note kept the sound's saturated colour after the caret pointed somewhere else and the sheet showed two selections at once. A note now wears that colour only while the tune is playing; a stopped playhead still moves, silently.
- Fixed a click on empty score paper leaving the target behind. abcjs only reports clicks it can match to a note, so the score container now lets go of the target on any click abcjs did not claim, in both panes at once.
- Fixed the caret and the score disagreeing about which note is being read. The caret is drawn against the character on its left, so the whitespace after a note now belongs to that note instead of jumping the target to the next one; the target moves only when the caret reaches the next note's first character. Play-from-cursor still reads a gap as "the note that has not sounded yet", which is a different question.
- Fixed play-from-cursor pressed right after typing: the request landed on the previous score's timing, and the re-render that followed paused the playback it had just started. A waiting edit is now rendered first, the request waits for the new synth, and one request runs at a time so a held shortcut cannot toggle playback back off.
- Fixed disabled header buttons looking active: icon buttons in the Source pane now dim when they cannot run.
- Fixed the playback highlight staying on the score, the keybed and the source after pausing. abcjs reports start, notes and the end of the tune but not pause, so the last sounding note kept its blue until the tune finished; the pause path now clears it.
- Fixed note-click source highlight that skipped the last note before a bar line. When seeking to a clicked note's timing position, abcjs could fire the next event instead of the clicked one. The highlight is now fired directly after seeking so the correct note is always highlighted.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-05-31

### Added

- Added "About ABC Desk" info dialog in Studio, featuring author information and handy links to MusicXML web converters.
- Added a provider-based ABC detector architecture with DeepSeek and Gemini fixture coverage and detector contribution notes.
- Rendered ABC scores now automatically wrap systems to the available score pane width, improving readability for long generated or converted ABC sources.
- Studio now has a source editor line-wrap toggle for long ABC lines.
- Studio playback now highlights the corresponding ABC source range while notes play.
- Studio now links to external MusicXML/ABC conversion tools instead of running an incomplete built-in MusicXML converter.

### Changed

- Restructured Studio Editor into a VS Code-style tier layout with a slim status and diagnostic footer instead of interleaving error panels in the content flow.
- Refined Studio UI spacing with a compact main header and editor toolbar spacing.

### Removed

- Built-in MusicXML/MXL import was removed so ABC Desk can stay focused on reliable ABC editing, validation, rendering, playback, and export workflows.

### Fixed

- ABC parser warning panels in rendered score cards now render as complete block panels instead of fragmented inline borders, and long abcjs messages wrap inside the card.
- Popup detection toggles now apply immediately to the active tab, including pages that were open before the extension loaded or reloaded.
- Playback progress bars now seek continuously while dragging, including before the first playback starts.
- Studio source editor now uses a light code background in light mode instead of keeping the dark editor palette.
- Studio editor toolbar status text now progressively hides at narrow pane widths so the action buttons stay aligned without adding vertical space.
- Studio split panes now keep narrow editor contents clipped to the editor pane instead of letting the source area overlap the preview.
- Studio ABC source editor now fills the available pane height when the quality panel is hidden.
- Playback no longer starts an automatic full-piano soundfont warmup that could leave abcjs note caches in a failed state after transient sample-load errors.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-05-26

### Added

- Independent Studio workspace (`src/studio`) for pasting, validating, and rendering ABC notation directly, with support for offline web deployment.
- Virtual piano keyboard sound interaction (click-to-play) via built-in soundfont.
- "Open in Studio" integration from content-script rendered scores to seamlessly move AI-generated music to the standalone editor.
- MIDI export from rendered score controls in chats and Studio.
- ABC source file import and export in Studio.
- Automatic ABC source warning checks in Studio using abcjs parser warnings, with a toggle to turn auto-checking off.
- Automatic ABC parser warning feedback in rendered score previews, with copyable repair prompts for AI chats.
- MusicXML import in Studio for converting `.musicxml`, `.xml`, and compressed `.mxl` scores into ABC.

### Changed
- Web and extension dual-build architecture (`vite.web.config.ts`), allowing Studio to be hosted as a standalone website.
